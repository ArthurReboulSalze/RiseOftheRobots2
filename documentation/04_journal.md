# 04 — Development journal

This is a chronological research log. Early interpretations are preserved here as history; documents 05, 07, 08, 09, and 10 describe the corrected current understanding. In particular, early claims that MVS held sound and MRW held video were disproved. The French originals remain in the local Git history; this public version records their findings in English.

## Session 26 — 2026-09-26: correcting fighter-effect attribution

An audio audit corrected the previous attribution of `FUN_226cc`. It writes the particle/effect state at `DAT_6852c`; it is not called once for every fighter animation frame. Its branches request either sample 3 from shared source bank 0 or sample 15 from a player bank. The latter is contextual particle audio, not evidence that every movement should play a whoosh. The port therefore removed the synthetic per-frame sample-3 callback.

`FUN_3a792` confirms that SOS looks up `sample_index` in the selected MRW bank through the u16 count and 8-byte offset/size directory. `FUN_150b4` loads distinct `R<slot>.MRS`/`R<slot>.MRW` pairs for the two selected fighters. In the explicit hit chain, `FUN_39996` requests sample 15 from source bank `attacker + 1`; the port consequently queues sample 15 from the attacking fighter's imported bank once per move, and re-arms that collision only when the fighter changes movement. This removes the repeated rapid impact sound caused by re-arming every 15 Hz simulation step.

The source bank is not a guarantee that every effect is unique: several sample-15 WAVs are byte-identical across distinct R-slot banks. That is intentional shared/category audio rather than a fallback to R0. To keep normal imports self-contained, the port now loads `EXTRACTED/audio/mrw/` directly, the 11,025 Hz WAVs produced by the importer, instead of relying on an untracked local `mrw_hq` resampling directory. SDL_mixer converts those chunks to its 44.1 kHz output device. MRS stream timing and pitch conversion still need a source-faithful implementation.

The in-game feature dig then refined the particle system (FUN_22848): five slots per player at 0x6852C+player*0x3C (stride 0xC: type u16, counter, index, cursor), 16 effect animations as two 8-pointer tables at 0x62702/0x62722 (type < 0x10 uses table one, type >= 0x10 uses table two indexed by type-0x10), each pointer targeting a u16 image-index list walked one entry per tick. No direct writers of the tables exist in the reference scan, so they are filled by block copy at load time; the lead is the STS files (28 files of 672 u16 words forming 86 FFFF-terminated segments — the same shape as frame-index lists), loaded alongside MVS by FUN_23e97. Linking the 16 tables to the STS segments and identifying the effect image bank remains open, as do the per-event sound triggers and the per-fight arena selection.

Audio was rebuilt on SDL_mixer 2.8.0 (added to thirdparty and the build): it fixes the failed raw-device open (audio device=0 in the log — SDL_OpenAudioDevice rejected the 11025 Hz U8 request; the mixer opens 44100 Hz stereo and converts chunks automatically), plays the hit WAV through Mix_PlayChannel, and streams the CD-rip MP3s — menu track 02 on the title and select screens, fight track 03 from match start, back to the menu track on quit-match. The nine rip tracks were copied into EXTRACTED/audio/cd/ for stable paths. Mapping tracks 03-10 to specific arenas/stages remains open, as does the original digital MGA-MGF music that the importer preserved for the Director's Cut profile.

The sound-call convention was read from assembly: FUN_3c680 receives EAX=sample index, EBX=pan, ECX=volume, EDX=pitch. FUN_226cc (animation-frame advance) plays sample 3 at pitches 0x5000/0x3000 and sample 15 with a variable pitch; FUN_39996 in the hit chain plays sample 15 at pitch 0x2000. The bank is the current fighter's own R<slot>.MRW, confirming per-robot sounds. All 1,142 samples were resampled to 44100 Hz 16-bit (EXTRACTED/audio/mrw_hq/), the fight loads each fighter's up-to-24 chunks at match start, and the hit plays the attacker's sample 15 (the bank attribution — attacker versus victim — is a first reading to tune by ear). Move-advance sounds (samples 3/15 with pitch variation) remain to wire.

The input bit semantics were wrong in the port and the builder FUN_197b6 settled them: the raw word (10 CFG keys per player, bits 0-9 player 1 and 10-19 player 2) maps to UP=0x08, DOWN=0x10, LEFT=0x04, RIGHT=0x02, punches 01/02/03 all OR into 0x01 (with hold-strength values 0x1E/0x3C/0x5A stored separately), kicks P1/P2/P3 all OR into 0x20. Inputs are absolute screen directions, never facing-relative; the movement sign handles facing. The MVS masks now read naturally: RIGHT->move 3 (walk +3), LEFT->move 2 (walk -3), UP->move 8 (jump), DOWN->move 7 (crouch), punch/kick bits->their attack moves. The user-reported misbehavior (up=advance, down=retreat, left/right=punches) matched the old wiring exactly. New default layouts per the user: player 1 arrows plus W/X/E punches and Q/S/D kicks; player 2 keypad (KP9/KP3/KP7/KP1 as the distinct diagonal codes) plus I/O/P and K/L/M; both stored in EXTRACTED/ui/rise2.cfg and visible in KEY MAPPING. The AI walks toward the player with absolute-direction bits. A user report that every robot shared the same hit sound traced to two stacked causes: their test ran an executable built before the per-fighter banks landed, and an older R0_04 queue call survived inside spawn_sparks, layering a constant sample over every hit. The leftover was removed. Fight music now picks randomly among the eight combat tracks (03-10) at match start, excluding the menu track 02, per the user's instruction. Note for parallel work: this file and the port sources receive edits from both agents; verify the on-disk build is current before diagnosing from stale logs (the log's bank names are RBT+slot, e.g. RBTL is the 640 bank of robot L, not a 320 bank). The user then reported no hit sound at all; the log had no per-fighter sound lines because the load_fighter_sounds CALLS had been lost to the same aborted-batch pattern (the definition survived, the two call sites did not). They were re-added through the Edit tool, and the per-robot hit sample (sample 15 of the attacker's bank) plus the log count line should now function. The recurring failure mode is confirmed: multi-replacement Python heredocs that assert late and write once at the end lose every replacement before the first failed assertion; use single-purpose edits or write incrementally. The user then paused further work pending a sound-only correction pass. ## Session 11 — 2026-09-22: locating the real samples

MRW, not MVS, is the sample-bank format. MRS drives sequences; SOUND.DAT configures audio hardware. The chain is `FUN_3be83` → `FUN_3a6b0` → `FUN_3a792` → `FUN_417c9` (SOS start call near `0x3a893`). MRW uses a u16 count and absolute (u32 offset, u32 size) pairs followed by unsigned 8-bit mono PCM, with `0x80` silence and an 11,025 Hz base rate. The original 69 banks yielded 1,142 WAV files; their spans cover about 99.8% of bank bytes. For R0 entry 0, offset `0x9a` and size 2,769 give a real waveform. Pitch adjusts playback rate as `(pitch_16_16 * 11025) >> 16`. `TOOLS/extract_mrw_audio.py` exports the banks.

## Sessions 9–10 — 2026-09-22: correcting MVS attribution

Playing presumed MVS audio at 11,025 Hz produced only noise; the user confirmed it by listening. Tracing the loader showed MVS is a 96-move-per-robot table, not an SOS codec. Each 32-byte descriptor contains three animation pointers, three displacement pointers, a transition pointer, and four control bytes. RBT0 idle at descriptor `0x190` and sequence `0xd90` matched the visible breathing animation. The early question “where are the samples?” was answered in session 11: MRW banks.

## Sessions 5–8bis — 2026-09-22: combat reverse engineering

The combat loop, player structure, MVS state machine, facing, physics, AI, collisions, damage, projectiles, particles, and background scrolling were mapped from `RISE2.EXR`. Important anchors include round controller `FUN_137ce`, frame update `FUN_2163a`, state dispatch `FUN_234fa`, physics `FUN_21baf`, facing `FUN_25615`, hit application `FUN_38b72`, AI `FUN_35a7c/35eb8`, and box comparison `FUN_3885e`. The player stride is 0x97, full health is 120, super meter caps at 24, and close-range threshold is 61 pixels. The exact formulas and uncertainties are in page 08.

`FUN_15d92` confirmed CL2 records: a 16-byte header, then three counts and 5/6/5-byte attack/body/single boxes. The original 28 robot CL2 files yield 9,204 frames, 1,119 attack boxes, 11,170 body boxes, and 7,091 single boxes. Box x/y scales are 4/2. `FUN_253a8` handles wall pushback, `FUN_244eb` three projectile slots per player, `FUN_22848` five particle slots, and `FUN_3a0d2` four background channels. Earlier claims that `FUN_25615` was a general frame step were corrected: it updates facing. The exact purpose of the third CL2 box group remains unresolved.

## Session 4 — 2026-09-22: first parsers, later corrected

The first `extract_data.py` parsers located AIP, STS, CTL, CL2, MRW, MVS, SOUND.DAT, `RISE2.CFG`, and high scores. ## Session 2bis — 2026-09-22: LZW and the resolved geometry block
